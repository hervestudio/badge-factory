00 / PREPARATION

Materials & tools

Use 28–30 AWG silicone wire. Cut for each route: making every wire 10–12 cm long leaves too much cable to close the enclosure. Close the halves like a book, hinging along the cable-window side. No wire should run over the ESP32. Group wires by destination with Kapton tape every 3 cm.

  • Four 100 kΩ resistors; four M2 brass inserts; four M2 × 12 mm countersunk screws. The 8.75 mm front requires 12 mm screws; 10 mm screws engage only about 1.2 mm of the inserts.
  • Steel strap bar, Ø3 × 29.4 mm; thin double-sided battery tape; cyanoacrylate glue for the caps.
  • Fine-tip soldering iron, solder, Ø1.5–2 mm heat-shrink tubing, wire stripper, knife for sacrificial membranes and an insert-setting iron.

See the component purchase list for supplier references. The Medium model here follows the guide’s 505060 battery, rather than the older 503040 entry in that list.

Never do these things
  • Do not connect the DevKit’s native USB port: GPIO 19/20 (D−/D+) are used for buttons. Flash over Wi-Fi OTA.
  • Never connect B+ directly to a GPIO: 4.2 V exceeds the 3.6 V absolute maximum. Use the 100 kΩ / 100 kΩ divider.
  • Respect the TP4056 battery polarity, B+ and B−. Reversing it destroys the protection.

Available hook-up wire colours: red, black, yellow, green and blue. Mark duplicate wires with a black marker ring near both ends; dashed lines in the original diagrams represent these identifying rings. The display uses a complete 10-way, 1.27 mm-pitch QWORK rainbow ribbon: each signal has its own colour, so no marker rings are needed.

GC9B72 display — four-wire SPI, ten-way ribbon

Solder the ribbon intact, in the module’s pad order. Keep it flat, without crossed conductors. The white SDO wire is not connected: it aligns with GPIO 46, which remains empty; trim it flush. The selected GPIOs cover consecutive header rows 13–20. Brown continues two rows farther to GND, skipping 5V; only red separates to reach 3V3 near the top of the header.

Badges wired before 14 August used SCLK 12 / MOSI 11 / DC 13 / RST 14. Rework those four wires at the ESP32 to the updated mapping below so all badges use the same firmware.

Stripboard — common ground and two dividers

Cut 5 strips × 8 holes (12.7 × 20.3 mm) from the Rademacher board. Score the hard-paper substrate along the holes with a knife, then snap it cleanly. The board fits the rimmed seat at the lower right of the front shell’s interior; secure it by friction or a small glue dot.

Lay the four resistor bodies flat, spanning neighbouring strips. The complete assembly is about 4 mm high. Upright resistors are about 11 mm high and exceed the 5.4 mm available below the back shell; this was confirmed during assembly on 13 August. Nothing should float loose inside the case.

Figure 3 — Button grounds join the central strip. The two dividers stay on the stripboard; only their two sensing wires travel to the ESP32. The original guide uses yellow/green for the ADC wires and blue for VBUS, with identifying rings where colours repeat.

R1 = R2 = R3 = R4 = 100 kΩ. The central GND strip keeps every resistor bridge one strip wide. Wires leave through the side notches of the seat. No capacitors are required: ADC filtering is handled in software, validated on the first two badges.

02 / AT THE BENCH

Assembly order

The sequence below preserves the source guide. The 3D story places final cable connection after the ESP32 so every destination is visible.

  1. Prepare the shells

    Open the seven sacrificial membranes in the front shell with a knife or fine screwdriver: three button counterbores and four screw seats. Heat-set the four M2 inserts into the rear posts at approximately 200 °C. Keep them vertical and flush.

  2. Prepare the wires

    Cut and tin each wire using the route lengths in section 00. Cut the five-strip, eight-hole board; solder the four resistors and lay them flat, keeping the total height around 4 mm. Solder its wires using the strip table, then clip or glue the board into its seat. Tin the display and TP4056 pads.

  3. Install the switches

    Insert each switch from outside through its Ø8.8 mm front opening into its cradle, with the plunger facing forwards. Bend the legs towards the back and through the support-plate slots. Solder only after insertion. Connect the three grounds to the shared bus and one signal wire to each switch. Route the bundle towards the upper-left window.

    Figure 4 — Button station cross-section: insert the switch from the front; folded legs pass through the support slots and are soldered from inside.

  4. Fit the display

    Place it in the circular seat with the connector tab pointing down into the notch. It rests on the lower stops on both sides. Solder the ten-way ribbon in pad order; leave white disconnected at the ESP32. Route it through the ESP wall windows.

  5. Install the TP4056

    Slide the board into the pocket at the bottom of the front shell, aligning USB-C with the edge opening. It rests on the support wall and fits beneath the two retaining stops. Solder B+, B−, OUT+ and OUT− through the central access window between the stops.

  6. Fit the battery in the rear shell

    Lay the pouch landscape on its shelf, above the screw posts, with tabs facing upwards. Secure it with double-sided tape. Route its leads to the TP4056 through the space above the battery.

  7. Fit the ESP32 in the rear shell

    The DevKit sits between its two retaining walls, located at the top under the strap header. Connect common GND, 5 V from OUT+, the display, buttons and sensing dividers, in that order. Keep all wires off the mating plane.

  8. Test before closing

    Use the checklist below. All checks can be performed with the enclosure open.

  9. Close the enclosure

    Put the Ø3 × 29.4 mm steel bar in the strap cradle spanning both halves. Bring the shells together like a book while tucking the wires inside. Secure with four M2 × 12 mm countersunk screws. Thread the strap through the slot and around the bar.

  10. Finish

    Glue the four screw caps into their recesses with cyanoacrylate, recessed squares visible. Put a small glue dot inside each button-cap sleeve and fit the three caps to the plungers. Check every button still clicks.

04 / FIRMWARE

Flash without a cable

Choose OTA flash mode in the menu, or hold Up during power-on. Connect to Wi-Fi badge-threejs, password threejs2026, then run:

pio run -e ota -t upload
# Badge IP: 192.168.4.1

To leave flash mode without uploading, hold the centre button.

Quick reminders: battery thickness must not exceed 5 mm. Speaker photo/menu pairing is configured in the firmware using tools/photo2header.py. The source guide estimates 8–10 hours of runtime with the 505060 battery and approximately 2½ hours to recharge at 1 A.
